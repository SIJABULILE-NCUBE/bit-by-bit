# Bit by Bit: Business Overview

**Tagline:** Own it bit by bit.

**One-line pitch:** Bit by Bit helps small South African shops run lay-bys digitally, so customers can pay off goods in instalments with no interest and no debt, and shop owners always know who has paid what.

---

## 1. The problem

Lay-by is one of the most common ways South African families buy what they need: school uniforms in January, clothes and toys for December, furniture, appliances and phones. The customer pays a deposit, the shop keeps the item aside, and the customer pays it off over a few months before taking it home.

In small and independent shops, lay-by still runs on **paper**: a card or a book with handwritten amounts and a stamp.

**Who has it:**
- **Customers:** they lose their lay-by card, forget how much they owe, don't know when the lay-by expires, and have no proof if the shop records a payment wrong.
- **Shop owners:** they page through books to find a customer, chase people who stopped paying, keep stock "on hold" that may never be collected, and spend time on arguments about balances.

**How often:** every week for the owner, and every payment day (usually month-end) for the customer.

**What it costs them today:**
- Customers: lost payments, disputes and stress, and they sometimes give up on a lay-by and lose track of their money.
- Owners: stock tied up on shelves, lost sales, hours of admin and damaged customer trust.

## 2. The customer

| | Who | Why they care |
|---|---|---|
| **User** | Customers buying on lay-by: mostly parents and working families | Always know their balance and due date, with proof of every payment |
| **Payer** | Small and independent retailers: clothing, school wear, furniture, appliance and phone shops (1 to 3 branches) | Less admin, fewer disputes, more completed lay-bys, stock freed up faster |

The **shop owner pays**. Customers use it for free.

## 3. The solution

**Bit by Bit is a lay-by management app: the shop creates a lay-by plan, the customer sees their balance and due dates on their phone, every payment is recorded with proof, and the owner sees which lay-bys are on track, overdue or ready to collect.**

## 4. The impact

If Bit by Bit succeeds:
- Families buy what they need **without taking on debt or interest**.
- Customers always have proof of what they've paid.
- Shops complete more lay-bys and free up stock that would otherwise sit on hold.

**How we measure it:**
| Metric | Target after 3 months |
|---|---|
| Lay-bys completed (paid off and collected) | Up from an estimated 60% to 80% |
| Payments made on or before the due date | 75% or more |
| Balance disputes reported by owners | Down 70% |
| Shops still active after 3 months | 70% or more |

## 5. Business model

- **Customers:** free, always.
- **Shops:**
  - **Starter (free):** up to 20 active lay-bys
  - **Growth (R149 a month):** unlimited lay-bys, reminders and an overdue report
  - **Multi-branch (R349 a month):** several branches and staff accounts
- **Later:** a small fee on in-app payments (e.g. PayFast or Ozow), and an optional "payment reliability record" that customers can share to show they pay on time.

**Simple maths:** 300 Growth shops x R149 = R44,700 a month in recurring revenue.

## 6. Go-to-market: the first 100 customers

1. **Walk-in pilots (shops 1 to 10):** visit school wear and clothing shops in local malls and town centres in **January**, when school-uniform lay-bys peak. Set them up in person.
2. **Customers pull shops in:** every customer gets a link to their lay-by. When they see it working, they ask other shops "do you use Bit by Bit?"
3. **Seasonal campaigns:** "Back to school" (Oct to Jan) and "December lay-by" (Aug to Dec) pushes, timed to when lay-by volume is highest.
4. **Business associations and retail groups:** local business chambers and informal-trader groups for bulk onboarding.
5. **Referral reward:** a shop that refers another shop gets a month of Growth free.

## 7. Competition

| Alternative | What it does | What it misses |
|---|---|---|
| Paper lay-by cards and books | Free and familiar | Easy to lose, no reminders, no proof for the customer, slow to search |
| Big retailers' in-house lay-by systems | Work well inside large chains | Not available to small and independent shops |
| General POS / invoicing software | Records sales and invoices | Not built for deposits, instalments, hold periods and collection |
| "Buy now, pay later" apps | Customer gets the item immediately | It is a form of credit, which can mean fees, credit checks and debt |
| Spreadsheets / WhatsApp | Flexible | Private to the owner, customers can't see their own balance |

**Why Bit by Bit wins:**
- **Built only for lay-by:** deposit, instalments, hold period, overdue and ready-to-collect are first-class features.
- **Debt-free by design:** no interest and no credit check. The customer takes the item home only once it is paid.
- **Both sides see the same record**, so balance disputes disappear.
- **A risk score for every lay-by:** shops see which customers are at risk of dropping off and can remind them early with one tap on WhatsApp.
- **Proof for everyone:** PDF receipts for every payment and a QR lay-by slip that replaces the paper card.
- **Motivation, not pressure:** progress milestones and on-time streaks help customers finish what they started.
- Works on any cheap Android phone, installs from the browser, uses little data, and speaks English, isiZulu and Sesotho.
- **Founder advantage:** over 10 years in credit risk and banking, with NCA and RE5 certifications. I understand payment behaviour, default risk and the rules that protect consumers.

## 8. Day-one Must features

1. Log-in with two roles: **Customer** and **Shop owner**
2. The shop owner creates a lay-by for a customer: item, price, deposit, term and instalment amount
3. The customer sees each of their lay-bys: amount paid, balance, next due date and progress
4. Payments: the customer uploads proof of payment (or the owner records a cash payment), and the owner confirms it
5. Owner dashboard: active, overdue and ready-to-collect lay-bys, and the total outstanding

## 9. Assumptions to validate

- Small shops will move from paper if setup takes under 10 minutes.
- Customers will check their lay-by on their phone if they get a link (no app download).
- Lay-by agreements fall under the Consumer Protection Act (section 62); the cancellation and refund rules need to be confirmed and built into the product.
